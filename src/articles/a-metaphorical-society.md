As we watch the Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games 2026, it is pertinent to revisit the Olympic year of 2020 (which got postponed to 2021 due to COVID), when we were witness to some mindboggling stuff in our lovely country- India, that is Bharat. (This following article was written in May 2020 amidst the pandemic)

Every Olympic year, the collective woes of 1.3 billion people come to the fore. The second most populous nation has the lowest rank in terms of medals per capita and so far, it has won only 28 medals in the 24 Olympic Games that it has participated in since 1900. Keeping this in mind, NITI Aayog came up with a 20 point plan to win 50 medals at the Olympics in 2024. This included targeting a group of priority sports with high winning potential. As our PM has asked to turn the COVID19 crisis into opportunity, the Gov’t seems to have discovered the exact procedure of finding the extraordinary talents to achieve the target of 50@2024. 

The modus operandi looks simple, that is, to observe carefully the plight of the people, especially the younger ones or rather the potential candidates, as they come out and experience the aftermath of a stringent lockdown influenced by a pandemic and pick out those who endure the hardships the best. Going by this yardstick, we can be hopeful of medals by looking at the prospective candidates cycling thousands of kilometers (Road cycling), walking hundreds of kilometers on foot (Race walking), living without food for days and still walking miles in the scorching sun ( perhaps for marathon or other diet controlled events?). We can even be hopeful of a medal in swimming as many have sustained the floods in Assam (yes Assam’s inundated by floods, though the ‘national media’ doesn’t really bother about it, the selection process must be on).

As the ‘success’ story of Jyoti Kumari who cycled 1200km came to light at least one medal in cycling is a done deal, at least that’s what the Indian Cycling Federation felt and decided to invite Jyoti for trials next month. "If she has achieved this, then she is really talented. We would like to give her an opportunity. We look for talent like this. If an international cyclist rides 100 km in a day, then it is called good training”, said VN Singh, Director of the Cycling Federation. 
Jyoti Kumari, a 15 year old native of Bihar’s Darbhanga, cycled almost 1,200 km with her wounded father riding pillion, to reach home from Gurgaon. Her father had met with an accident and got injured so couldn’t continue driving autorikshaw. As the lockdowns were extended, they were left with no money to pay rent and the landlord cut the power twice wanting to throw them out and also ran out of food. They were left with no other choice but to go home to their village. She convinced her father somehow and set out on her bicycle, which she had got from an acquaintance for Rs.1000. Braving the hardships, she somehow reached her village after cycling for 10 days!

When we come across such a tale of woe, the primary response that we would expect from a democratic country based on welfare ideals is a sense of grief and regret. But what we got along with this report are these- CFI called it an “achievement”, Union minister Ashwini Kumar Choubey said “will consider making her health ambassador and spread the message of the importance of courage and fitness”, Law minister Ravi Shankar wrote “moved to see the courage of young girl”, LJP President Chirag Paswan recommended her for the President’s award for courage, Bihar minister Madan Sahni honoured her with shawl, garland and gifted her clothes, MLA Sanjay Saroj gifted her a brand new cycle and fed her sweets. Perhaps the best compliment came from Ms Ivanka Trump, as she was excited to see the “beautiful feat of endurance & love that has captured the imagination of the Indian people”.

It might be a beautiful feat of endurance for Ms.Ivanka tweeting from America but seeing such reactions from the leaders of our own country signals a fundamental flaw in our society. Democracy is considered as the superior form of Governance not because it is infallible per se, but it provides the channels for introspection. It is a society which offers a mode of associated living, of conjoint communicated experience, as remarked by John Dewey. Politics is a game of perceptions, which the politicians form as well as represent, as a mirror to the society. This episode is an archetype of the trenchant class divisions, where one section (majority) is forced to paddle thousands of kilometers due to hunger and poverty while the other (elite) watch it in awe as a feat of bravery and fitness. The predicament of Jyoti Kumari should be a moment of introspection for us as a society, asking why she was forced to go through that in the first place. As Bong Joon-ho’s Parasite portrays, how metaphoric the human lives are, right?
