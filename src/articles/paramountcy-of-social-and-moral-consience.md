genre: Philosophy

Constitutionalism is the cornerstone of democracy. It secures the necessary checks and balances required as impedance to the usurpation of power by the executive. All the four pillars of democracy (media included, optimistically) should realize their constitutional obligation and uphold the constitutional morality. But for the past few years the hope from the executive (and legislature, with brute majority) to uphold the constitutional ethos has completely diminished, turning the democracy into a virtual majoritarianism. In such circumstances, the voices that strive to safeguard the constitution and protect the ‘Idea of India’ have no choice but to prostrate before the Supreme Court as the last savior. But what we all fail to understand is, the Supreme court is also a subset of the ‘State’, which is also influenced by the day to day happenings and evolve along with the society. The State progresses collectively and not in isolation. Thus what matters is the ‘moral and social conscience’ of the society at large, which is the driving force for any change legally. The parliament makes laws reflecting the social consciousness of the present condition of the State, confident of securing the support of its core vote bank and a significant number of people. For example, the parliament can’t and won’t pass a law legalizing sati at this point of time, because social conscience of the State abhors the practice of sati, as we have progressed as human beings to think rationally and understand that the practice of sati is inhuman and irrational. Similarly the Supreme Court functions within that social conscience and is bound to get influenced by the burgeoning majoritarianism.

As German sociologist Ulrich Beck propounded, we live in what is called as the ‘risk society’ and ‘second modernity’. In this information age, people are immensely influenced by misinformation and disinformation, thereby filled with a sense of insecurity and uncertainty.  People are easily susceptible to fake news and fear mongering as they lack the social solidarity and mutual trust and thus feel isolated individually in this post-industrial age. This paves the way for the rise of right wing populism worldwide where the leaders could exploit the people emotionally, catering to their conservative ideas like race, religion, caste, ethnicity, xenophobia. This is exacerbated by deep penetration of social media and unauthorized news sources, allowing us to embrace information that confirms our biases. This has resulted in “Democracy devouring itself” as Professor Shawn W Rosenberg has concluded in his research paper. Taking democracy’s place, Rosenberg says, will be right-wing populist governments that offer voters simple answers to complicated questions. Democracy requires thoughtfulness, discipline and logic. It requires consistent discourse to be built upon rationality, humanism and truthfulness from the democratic voices by engaging with the public at large directly to develop the collective conscience of the society.

The best we could hope for in the short term is, that the Supreme court would adhere to the constitutional morality and exercise its responsibility to impede the juggernaut of right wing populism. Irrespective of the Judiciary’s role, we as the people of India should have the moral responsibility to counter the undemocratic forces. As Helen Keller remarked, “Until the great mass of the people shall be filled with the sense of responsibility for each other's welfare, social justice can never be attained”. At the turn of this decade we shall vow to work towards developing the moral and social conscience of the society, to uphold the constitutional ethos and to march forward as the progressive forces embracing humanism. Failing in which may even lead to despising secularism, justifying casteism, asserting racial supremacy and culminating in the decimation of the ‘Idea of India’ that our founding fathers envisaged. Change does not roll in on the wheels of inevitability, but comes through continuous struggle. Let each one of us be the agent of change, socially and politically, to cherish the ideals of our constitution and practice it in letter and spirit.
